In Ireland (Republic of Ireland), **Subject Access Requests (SARs)** are governed by the **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)** and the **Data Protection Act 2018**. These rights allow individuals to request access to their personal data held by organizations.

Here’s a breakdown of what can typically be **fully requested/received**, **partially requested/received**, and **not requested/received** under SARs in Ireland:

---

## **1. What Can Be Fully Requested/Received**
You can request and expect to receive:

### **Personal Data**
- **Basic identity information**: Name, address, date of birth, contact details.
- **Employment records**: Job title, salary, employment history, performance reviews, disciplinary records (if related to you).
- **Educational records**: Qualifications, grades, attendance records.
- **Financial records**: Bank details, transaction history, credit scores (if held by the organization).
- **Health records**: Medical reports, prescriptions, treatment history (if held by the organization, e.g., an employer or healthcare provider).
- **Online activity**: Social media interactions, emails, chat logs, browsing history (if held by the organization).
- **Biometric/genetic data**: Fingerprints, DNA profiles, facial recognition data (if held by the organization).

### **Data Processing Information**
- **Purpose of processing**: Why your data is being used.
- **Categories of data**: Types of personal data processed.
- **Recipients of data**: Who your data is shared with (including third parties).
- **Retention periods**: How long your data is kept.
- **Automated decision-making**: If your data is used for profiling or automated decisions, you can request information about the logic involved.

---

## **2. What Can Be Partially Requested/Received**
Some information may be **partially available** or require redaction:

### **Third-Party Data**
- If your data includes information about others (e.g., references, witness statements, or data from colleagues), the organization may **redact or withhold** this to protect the rights of others.

### **Legal Privilege**
- **Confidential legal advice**: Communications between a lawyer and their client are protected and may not be disclosed.
- **Litigation documents**: If the data is part of ongoing or anticipated legal proceedings, it may be exempt.

### **Commercial Sensitivity**
- **Trade secrets**: Information that could harm the organization’s competitive position may be withheld.
- **Proprietary algorithms**: If your data is processed using unique algorithms or methods, the logic may not be fully disclosed.

### **Public Interest Overrides**
- **National security**: Data related to national security may be exempt.
- **Crime prevention**: Data processed for preventing or detecting crime may be withheld if disclosure could compromise investigations.

---

## **3. What Cannot Be Requested/Received**
The following are **not typically accessible** via SARs:

### **Exempt Data**
- **Exemptions under GDPR/DP Act 2018**:
  - Data processed for **journalistic, artistic, or literary purposes** (if the organization is a media outlet).
  - Data processed for **research or statistical purposes** (if disclosure could impair the research).
  - Data processed for **archiving in the public interest**.
  - Data processed for **defense or national security**.

### **Opinion Data**
- **Subjective opinions**: Data consisting solely of the opinions of others about you (e.g., gossip, rumors) may not be disclosed if it could cause harm.

### **Third-Party Identities**
- **Identities of whistleblowers or informants**: If revealing their identity could put them at risk.
- **Confidential references**: References provided for jobs, education, or other purposes are often exempt.

### **Ongoing Investigations**
- Data related to **active criminal or disciplinary investigations** may be withheld until the investigation is complete.

---
### **Key Legal Framework**
- **GDPR (Articles 12–15)**: Outlines the right to access personal data.
- **Data Protection Act 2018 (Section 60)**: Implements GDPR in Ireland and includes additional exemptions.
- **Data Protection Commission (DPC)**: The Irish regulator for data protection complaints.

---
### **Practical Steps for SARs in Ireland**
1. **Submit a written request** to the organization holding your data. Include:
   - Your name, address, and contact details.
   - A clear description of the data you’re requesting.
   - Proof of identity (e.g., copy of passport or driver’s license).
2. **Organization’s response time**: They must respond within **1 month** (extendable to 3 months for complex requests).
3. **Fees**: SARs are usually free, but organizations can charge a “reasonable fee” for excessive or repetitive requests.
4. **Complaint to DPC**: If you’re unsatisfied with the response, you can escalate to the [Data Protection Commission](https://www.dataprotection.ie).
